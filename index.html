Atomic Games

Playable explanations of real systems

Prose tells you the rules. A simulation lets you poke them. Only a game makes you commit to a belief and pay for being wrong — and that payment is where conceptual understanding actually comes from.

Each game here takes one system, finds the part of it you cannot see, puts a lever on it, and then makes winning impossible without understanding it. They are single HTML files: no build step, no network, no tracking, no storage. Open one and it runs.

How they are built

With the generalized Atomic Intrinsic Integration method. The original was defined by Echeverría, Barrios, Nussbaum, Améstica and Leclerc for games teaching conceptual physics, fusing intrinsic integration (Habgood & Ainsworth) with an atomic analysis of game structure (Koster, Cousins, Cook), and validated experimentally against a non-AII version of the same game. This repository uses a generalization of it from physical laws to arbitrary systems — codecs, protocols, markets, caches, supply chains.

The short version:

  1. Model the system — levers, mechanisms, readouts, coupling, and the conserved quantity nobody can conjure more of.
  2. Write down the misconceptions. They are the real specification: a level that cannot be lost by someone holding the wrong belief is not attacking anything.
  3. Atomize — one lever per low-level atom, one readout per mechanism, the coupling drawn beside its parts.
  4. Put the learning on the critical path to winning. Optional learning is skipped learning.
  5. Scaffold the invisible state, then fade it — present, degraded, absent, inverted. Scaffolding left in place is a trap: players beat the game by trial and error and learn nothing.
  6. Charge a Reflection Tax. A challenge is doing its job when a wrong action costs more than a moment of thought.

Two rules these games keep

  • Simulate the mechanism; never fake the readout. If a bar says “bits”, something counted bits. You are calibrating real intuition against these numbers, and a plausible formula invented to make a graph move would quietly miscalibrate you.
  • State what the model omits, in prose, inside the artifact. A caricature presented as the truth is the documented ethical failure of simulation explorables.
